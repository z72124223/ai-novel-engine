## 4. Desired emotional rhythm

The user wants a rhythm that lowers the barrier to attachment through humor, daily life, warmth and character chemistry, then earns anger, reflection, grief or tears through consequences.

The desired wave is approximately:

```text
funny／lively
→ daily friction and affection
→ warmth／friendship／romance
→ unease
→ conflict escalation
→ value dilemma
→ anger／loss／reflection
→ changed relationship
→ partial relief and new hope
```

The user referenced high-level emotional experiences associated with works such as 活俠傳 and Gundam SEED, but the project must not copy protected characters, dialogue, plot or signature expression. The inspiration is pacing, contrast, ensemble investment and growth.

The reader should first care about the characters. Tragedy matters only when warmth, laughter, romance, friendship, home and ordinary habits have made those characters emotionally real.

## 5. Protagonist growth

The user wants the protagonist to grow until he can influence major conflicts and potentially turn the tide of war.

## 6. No single final ending

The user dislikes the feeling that a beautiful or tragic ending means never seeing the characters again.

The project therefore adopts:

```text
local closure + persistent world continuation
```

Each arc must still:

- pay off promises;
- resolve a meaningful problem;
- alter relationships;
- leave permanent state;
- complete a character phase;
- open a new life question.

“No final ending” must not become endless repetition, unresolved setup, escalating villains without meaning or reset consequences.

## 7. New protagonist voice: sarcasm and banter

The user proposed giving Lu Chenxing a sharper sarcastic／commentary voice.

Reasons:

- Viewers often identify obvious horror-film danger before characters do;
- Stories lose credibility when characters repeatedly ignore clear risk;
- A perceptive protagonist can voice the audience’s concern;
- Sarcasm can reduce predictability, reveal intelligence and create emotional attachment;
- Humor can make later seriousness more powerful.

The design is not “constant meme character.” It includes:

```text
DAILY_QUIP
DANGER_OBSERVATION
GRIEF_RESTRAINT
```

Lu may notice obvious risk, question absurd orders, call out contradictions and use self-deprecation. He cannot know author-only facts, cannot become fourth-wall omniscient and cannot always be correct.

His humor must have consequences:

- build friendship or romance;
- offend someone;
- expose fear;
- challenge authority;
- help a group survive pressure;
- become unavailable when grief or moral weight is too large.

The voice remains a non-Canon REBOOT candidate until user review after real play evidence.

## 8. Predictability and fair surprise

The user observed that audiences abandoning a story often comes from predicting every beat.

The solution is not random twists. The project adopts `fair_surprise`:

```text
visible setup
→ audience and smart character form a reasonable first-order prediction
→ character recognizes and responds to obvious danger
→ first danger is avoided or reduced
→ opponent/world adapts
→ second-order consequence surprises but remains causal
→ hindsight confirms the clues
```

The system should track:

- audiencePredictionRisk;
- IDIOT_PLOT_RISK;
- cheapTwistRisk;
- fairForeshadowing;
- reversalFairness;
- antagonistAdaptation;
- secondOrderConsequence;
- genreAwarenessWithoutOmniscience;
- banterNaturalness;
- voiceDistinctiveness.

Characters may enter danger only with a defensible reason such as missing information, urgency, moral duty, relationship pressure, coercion, resource constraint, false belief or calculated tradeoff.
